A Virtual Canvas of Botanical Dreams

Spolia botanique invites architecture students, along with a wider public, to compose a virtual canvas of botanical dreams woven into the streets and squares of contemporary cities. Through playful mobile applications and immersive experiences, the project fosters forms of collaboration on a global scale while opening a space for reflection on the botano-digital future of urban environments. It calls forth the latent vegetal life concealed within the mineral fabric of the city, sketching the horizon of a chlorophyll-infused future in which floral imagination begins to permeate both architecture and technological thought.

These experimental applications allow users, wherever they are in the world, to transform their immediate surroundings into a vegetal utopia and to explore it through augmented reality. Equipped with a smartphone, each participant can enter this expanded form of urban space through an “augmented botanical walk,” where physical and virtual environments converge, and where botanical life and digital systems become deeply entangled.

The project seeks to provide inhabitants with an intuitive means of shaping the urban imaginary of their own city. Within this perspective, the figure of the metropolitan resident is redefined through the dual lens of Homo Ludens and Homo Faber, the human who plays and the human who makes, as articulated in utopian thought. Urban space thus emerges as a field of creative action and playful exchange.

In architecture, spolia designates the reuse of fragments taken from existing buildings and reassembled within new constructions. Here, the term is extended into a botanical and virtual domain, where the vegetal world is captured through meticulous 3D scanning and reconstituted as digital matter. These virtual gardens, assembled piece by piece, become a new kind of resource, akin to brick or stone, through which the city is reimagined as an immersive and shared botanical environment.

The project was developed in partnership with the Domaine de Chaumont-sur-Loire, the École nationale supérieure d’architecture de Paris-Belleville, and the joint research unit AUSser. The school functioned as a laboratory for experimentation, engaging students in the process, while AUSser ensured the scientific dissemination and articulation of the results. The festival offered a singular terrain for gathering the vegetal material that underpins the project.

In 2022, Comet Lab was selected as one of twenty winners invited to design a garden for the Festival International des Jardins, hosted by the Domaine de Chaumont-sur-Loire. In tandem with this project, we partnered with the Domaine to develop a second initiative: Spolia botanique. This project was designed as an artistic and digital intervention set within the festival gardens.

Since 1992, the Festival International des Jardins at Chaumont-sur-Loire has served as a premier global laboratory for contemporary garden design and landscape architecture. As a vital incubator for new ideas, the festival constantly revitalizes the art of gardening. It engages both the public and industry professionals by introducing unique plant varieties, innovative materials, and visionary concepts.

Domaine de Chaumont-sur-Loire,
Centre-Val de Loire Region.

The Jardin d'Eaurmus, designed for this festival, featured a pathway centered around a menhir-like fountain. This structure generated a series of vortices in the flow of water in order to re-energize it. Like the nineteen other temporary gardens, it was dismantled after six months to make way for new creations by the following year's winners.

To preserve these ephemeral works, we used 3D scanning to digitize selected segments, effectively building a three-dimensional botanical archive. These high-resolution scans capture the rich plant and mineral details with remarkable precision. We then integrated the resulting virtual fragments into the Festum Virtual application, allowing for an immersive exploration of these environments.

Digital Craft:
A Doctoral Inquiry

The Spolia botanique project explores the concept of digital craftsmanship within the field of architecture, viewed as a convergence of the artisanal age and the digital era. This approach aligns with contemporary theoretical frameworks such as postmodern medievalism, digital archaism, and the primitive future, as explored by thinkers like Régis Debray and Jacques Lucan, and by architects such as Sou Fujimoto and Toyo Ito.

This line of inquiry is also central to the doctoral research of Mete Kutlu, the project lead and a former resident artist of the Villa Medici. He developed these ideas in his article "A Trans-Temporal Inquiry in Architecture," published in Culture et Recherche (no. 141, 2020). The project serves as a significant case study within his dissertation, which he defended in November 2025.

Traditionally, architects design projects using small-scale representations, often working within a studio far from the construction site. Our augmented reality application changes this dynamic by enabling design directly on-site and at full scale. The architect becomes immersed in the environment they are creating, establishing a more immediate and embodied relationship with the context. This aligns the design process with the spatial and sensory reality of the site.

The use of augmented reality reactivates the intuitive, hands-on bond that the medieval master builder shared with the construction site. It reimagines the site as a collaborative space where artisans worked and invisible imaginaries intersected. This technological shift challenges the architectural paradigm established during the Italian Renaissance, where the architect moved their imagination onto paper through systematic and abstract drawings.

Immersive Design

Comet Lab offers architecture students a mobile application that enables them to design botanical and virtual augmentations of urban spaces. Through this platform, we seek to explore the following questions:

How can immersive design be developed through augmented reality tools to conceive future habitats where physical and virtual spaces are seamlessly interwoven?

How can contemporary cities be transformed into utopian urban scenarios, enhanced by suspended virtual gardens?

How might we imagine a virtual extension of a physical building; for example, a new digital façade for a Haussmannian structure, or a botanical urban skin that is variable, adaptable, and customizable according to the desires of each inhabitant?

The experience of our immersive design application serves as a crucial intellectual exercise for anticipating how the architect's role and tools will evolve in the age of artificial intelligence. It requires thinking within a hybrid and unprecedented environment, situated at the threshold between the physical world and the virtual domain.

The primary challenge lies in the need for precise integration of virtual constructions with existing buildings and urban settings. This condition compels the architect to operate simultaneously across both realms, perceiving virtual space as a direct extension of the physical environment. Ultimately, the goal is to conceive of these two domains as a fluid and symbiotic continuum.

The Team

Mete Kutlu
Project lead, creative technologist, architect and researcher in cosmotechnics with a PhD from Université Gustave Eiffel. His work operates at the intersection of spatial computation, visual cultures, and cosmology, with a focus on augmented reality and algorithmic processes. His doctoral research examines how premodern visual systems and contemporary digital technologies converge in the construction of space across architectural and cosmic scales. A former fellow of Villa Medici, the French School in Rome, the French Institute of Anatolian Studies, the French Institute of Central Asian Studies, and the Fondazione Giorgio Cini, he has conducted research across the Mediterranean and the Silk Road, and currently teaches at ENSA Paris-Belleville.
